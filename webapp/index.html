1. Mathematical Formulation of PDI

For each detected antral follicle with centroid $\mathbf{f}_i = (x_i, y_i)$, the normalized distance from the ovarian centroid $\mathbf{c} = (x_c, y_c)$ along ray angle $\theta_i = \text{atan2}(y_i - y_c, x_i - x_c)$ is defined as:

$$\rho_i = \frac{\|\mathbf{f}_i - \mathbf{c}\|_2}{R(\theta_i)}$$

where $R(\theta_i)$ is the exact distance from the ovarian centroid to the outer ovarian capsule boundary along vector $\mathbf{u}(\theta_i)$. The global Peripheral Dispersion Index is the average normalized distance:

$$\text{PDI} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \rho_i, \quad \text{PRC}_{\ge 0.65} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{I}(\rho_i \ge 0.65)$$

The Critical Discovery: Shortcut Learning in Public Datasets

During our empirical investigation of the widely-used pcos-xai-ultrasound-dataset, we uncovered two critical flaws that invalidate dozens of recently published papers:

  1. Severe Image Duplication (Data Leakage): Out of 5,000 "noninfected" images, there are only 812 unique MD5 hashes—an 83.8% duplication rate causing severe cross-validation leakage.
  2. Anatomical Organ Mislabeling: The "control" cohort consists entirely of wide-angle transvaginal scans of the uterine corpus and endometrium (578×850 px), rather than normal ovaries!
  3. The Black-Box Failure: Deep CNNs reporting "99.8% test accuracy" merely classified Ovary vs. Uterus based on probe aspect ratio and myometrial striation, failing completely as clinical PCOS diagnostic models.
